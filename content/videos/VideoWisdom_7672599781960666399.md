## Summary (1 paragraph)

A 49-second oracle dialogue — *Canticle of the Hollow*'s "Keep Your Eggs" — makes an egg a test of knowing. "Keep your eggs. From whom? Anyone who says they know what they're for." The student's certainty — "I know what this one is for. I'm going to hatch a guard" — is precisely "how they get you. Who? Gods." She has had three: one became a king, one a religion, one moved back home — the third undercutting grandeur with bathos. The student's binary — "Then should I break it? That gives it a destiny. Should I keep it? That gives it power" — is refused as unhelpful oracle logic, which is why "they stop calling women like me oracles. What do they call you now? Old." The final turn — "And what will they call me? Whatever they need from you" — reveals the egg's power is not in hatching or breaking, but in remaining unassigned, and the oracle's age is the price of refusing to tell people what their eggs are for.

## Key Insights (Distilled)

1. **Keep from knowers.** "Anyone who says they know what they're for" is the predator — certainty about purpose is the trap.
2. **Hatch a guard as self-fulfilling.** Knowing "I'm going to hatch a guard" is how gods get you — naming the future enlists you in it.
3. **Three eggs, three fates.** King, religion, moved back home — grandeur, institution, and anti-climax are equally possible from same egg; the joke is that religion is the one she's joking about.
4. **Break = destiny, keep = power.** Both actions give the egg meaning; the oracle refuses the binary — not breaking or keeping is the third way.
5. **Why oracles become "old."** Refusing to be helpful (to tell you what to do) gets women un-oracled and renamed old — helpfulness is the price of the title.
6. **You will be called whatever they need.** The student's future name is not identity but utility — the hollow is filled by others' need.

## Mental Models

- **Egg-Knower Filter:** Who says they know what your egg is for? Keep it from them first.
- **Guard-Hatch Trap:** Declaring "I will hatch a guard" creates the guard you fear — watch self-fulfilling naming.
- **Break/Keep Double Bind:** Both give power/destiny — consider a third: *keep without assigning*.
- **Oracle → Old:** Refusing to be helpfully predictive costs the title but keeps integrity.
- **Whatever They Need:** Your name after will be their need, not your essence — choose which need you will serve by remaining unassigned.

## Contrarian Take (Required by ExtractWisdom)

The oracle's "keep your eggs from anyone who knows what they're for" is wise, but taken literally it becomes hoarding — some eggs *do* need to be hatched, broken, or given away, and "whatever they need from you" is not always exploitation; sometimes it's vocation. The canticle's refusal to be helpful risks becoming a mystique that withholds the very guidance the student came for — the old oracle may be old precisely because she stopped being useful when usefulness was needed.
