2. **Runaway becomes priest by murder.** He comes with nothing, breaks the tree, kills the guardian — priesthood is not anointment but usurpation.
3. **King of the Wood as countdown.** "They called him a king, but the crown was a countdown. The hunt for him started the hour he won it." — kingship begins its own hunt.
4. **Staying awake as tenure.** He keeps it "as long as he could stay awake" — vigilance, not legitimacy, holds the crown.
5. **Goddess doesn't learn names.** A thousand years, a hundred kings, no names needed — the game, not the man, is the constant.
6. **Rite of scattering.** "The man who is ripped apart and scattered so the year comes back around" — Frazer's King of the Wood/Diana at Nemi — death ensures fertility.

## Contrarian Take (Required by ExtractWisdom)

The canticle's "game doesn't change, only the man" risks fatalism — every King of the Wood is interchangeable, so no king matters, and the grove's violence becomes aesthetic. But some groves *do* change — Diana's mirror can be drained, the tree can be cut, the priesthood can be ended — and not every runaway should become priest by murder; sometimes the most sacred act is to leave the grove unguarded, break no bow, and let the next man with nothing keep walking past.
