## Key Insights (Distilled)

1. **Two-way abandonment, one flight.** Both claim abandonment, but only one fled — the child's flight makes abandonment a story, then a religion.
2. **Religion of world-taking.** "Believing whoever leaves you takes the world with them" becomes faith when a child, for whom the claim is true, carries it into adulthood where it is false.
3. **More than one world.** Growing is learning there is more than one world — and daring the heresy that there is more than one mother.
4. **Blood hates that question.** Kinship as cosmology resists plural mothers/worlds — the question itself is taboo.
5. **Standing as proof.** "Aren't you standing here?" — survival is evidence that multiplicity is true; you outlived the world you thought you were.
6. **Mistaking mother for universe.** First hurt endures because smallness made her universe-sized; scale, not malice, made loss total.
7. **World can end without you ending.** Maturity is knowing a world can end and you can remain — hollow but still standing.

## Contrarian Take (Required by ExtractWisdom)

The canticle's "more than one world/mother" is liberating, but risks spiritualizing abandonment — sometimes you *did* just abandon your mother, and she *did* just abandon you, and no archetype makes that less true. "A world can end without you ending" can become a bypass that congratulates standing while the hollow still needs grieving, not just knowing. The missing step is *which* world should end and *which* mother should be kept — multiplicity is not the same as healing.
