## Key Insights (Distilled)

1. **Not grieving, but striking.** "They said I was grieving, I was not" — reframes Demeter's famine as intentional work stoppage, not sorrow.
2. **Taking without address.** Daughter taken midday, no warning/negotiation/return address — abduction as administrative act (Hades/Zeus) that expects no reply.
3. **Put down the harvest.** "I was done feeding the world" — the one who feeds can un-feed; harvest is conditional labor, not endless gift.
4. **Famine as question, not disaster.** "They called it famine, I called it a question" — same field, two names: starvation vs. inquiry.
5. **They always come.** Withdrawal works — "they came to me eventually, they always come" — power comes to the one who stops feeding.
6. **Return my daughter, then harvest.** Negotiation is explicit: daughter for harvest, person for season — winter is the table, not the weather.
7. **Winter as ultimatum.** "They called it winter, it was an ultimatum." — seasonality mythologized to hide maternal strike and hostage negotiation.

## Mental Models

- **Question, Not Famine:** Is this famine or a question you haven't answered? Rename the field.

## Contrarian Take (Required by ExtractWisdom)

The canticle's "I was not grieving" is powerful as strike, but risks sanctifying withdrawal that starves the innocent — the fields that died were not Hades' fields, but mortals' who never took the daughter. Demeter's question came at the cost of famine for those with no return address either. The missing ethic is *who* pays for the question, and whether "winter as ultimatum" justifies the hunger of those who never sat at the negotiation table.
