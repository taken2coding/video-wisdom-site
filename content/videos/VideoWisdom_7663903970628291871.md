## Key Insights (Distilled)

1. **There when you couldn't be.** Death's first credential is presence in your absence — she was there for the loved ones you missed.
2. **Hated for presence.** Being hated your whole life for showing up is the reaper's lot — and she accepts it as universal.
3. **Not mine — tsars, fevers, orders.** The field's dead are not hers; history, disease, and bureaucracy do the killing — she merely catches.
4. **Life leaves, Death shows up.** Inversion: life is the active leaver, death is the passive catcher who shows up when life lets go — the untold part.
5. **Thief who stole nothing.** Called thief, but "nothing was ever stolen" — her appointment cannot be broken, only kept, and the missed appointments are also hers.
6. **Gentle at the end.** The needed truth: "I was gentle. They were not afraid at the end." — hate coexists with need to hear gentleness.
7. **Gentlest thing in the room, even when hated.** The final promise holds hate and gentleness together — when your turn comes, she will still be gentle, regardless of your lifelong hate.

## Contrarian Take (Required by ExtractWisdom)

The canticle's gentle Death is comforting, but risks absolving real takers — tsars *did* order, fevers *were* neglected, and "I catch them" can sound like a clean conscience that lets orders in warm rooms off the hook. Some deaths *are* stolen, and some appointments *are* broken by systems that could have caught differently. Gentleness at the end doesn't erase the field's cause, and "nothing was stolen" can be true metaphysically while still being false politically.
