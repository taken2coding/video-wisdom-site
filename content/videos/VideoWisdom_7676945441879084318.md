## Contrarian Take (Required by ExtractWisdom)

The canticle beautifies being chosen as pattern, but not every maze is a perfect fit made for you — some mazes are just bad design, and "I measured you before you knew how to stand" can sanctify grooming as destiny. The dialogue never asks if the offering *consented* to be for "what came before you," nor if the center that returns you is the same you who entered. Sometimes the part that could still choose is better served by *breaking* the pattern, not walking it to the center to be returned as someone else's devotion.

## Afterthought

**Center as Return: Center is not escape, but where the taken "could" is returned — labyrinth as reclamation.**

After *Center as Return* there is no escape, only restitution.

In that *Canticle*, the maze doesn't want your strength, it wants *the part of you that could still choose* and takes *everything that remembers you ever could* — your archive of alternative selves — until nothing is left and the world calls that *devoted*.

**Center reverses that transaction:**

- **Labyrinth as reclamation, not exit:** You don't walk *out* — you walk *in* to where the taken *could* is stored. Theseus doesn't escape the labyrinth, he reaches the Minotaur (center) and returns *with* the thread. Here, the maker who measured you *before you knew how to stand* reaches center and *you* are the thread returned.

- **Returned, not freed:** *You will be returned* is passive — someone *returns* you. You don't reclaim yourself; the pattern that bound you by first holding you restores you. Devotion was the interim name for emptiness; return is the end of that name.

- **What returns is the chooser:** Not the old *could*s as options, but the *faculty* that could choose — the part the maze wanted. After center you get back the chooser, but emptied of its memories of *could*, so it chooses without nostalgia.

In the Hollow's logic, after is not *outside* — it's *held again*, but now by the center, not the rope. If the first holding was capture, the second is custody.
