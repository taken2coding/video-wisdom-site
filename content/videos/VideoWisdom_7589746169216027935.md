## Summary (1 paragraph)

This is a blunt authenticity sermon built around a single inversion: you're not afraid because "that's not me" — you're afraid because it *is* you. Using his friend Cooper (a talented videographer hiding behind "that's not me"), Harry Miller argues fear marks identity, not distance. Putting out a "not-you" effigy to burn is safe; putting out the real you risks being hung. He links this to Radiohead's "How to Disappear Completely" ("that's not me" is how you disappear), glass-frog transparency (art requires being so clear they see what you ate for lunch), and the Sanskrit *Tat Tvam Asi* — thou art that — unpacked across first person (*I am that I am*, God / Cody's "let me be me"), second person (*thou art that*), and third person (*he is him* — you don't become someone else, you become you when the not-you falls away). Cyrano, *Let It Shine*, and Eminem's "The Real Slim Shady" become parables for the same law: pretending not to be you is a tragedy; resonance only happens at your natural frequency — shake at someone else's frequency and your amplitude collapses.

## Key Insights (Distilled)

1. **Fear signals identity, not alienness.** "You're not afraid because it's not you, you're afraid because it *is* you." Avoidance is a compass, not a disclaimer.
2. **Effigy vs. self.** Burning a fake "not-you" feels safe because nothing real is at stake; visibility with the real you feels lethal because it *is* real.
3. **"That's not me" is disappearance tech.** From Radiohead to any "that's not me" deflection, invisibility is not transparency — transparency is being seen through *to* something real, invisibility is being seen through to nothing.
4. **Glass-frog principle.** Great art requires such honesty that your skin/bones are clear — people see what you ate for lunch. Transparency is not oversharing, it's permeability.
5. **Tat Tvam Asi in three persons.** First person *I am that I am* (God), *let me be me* (Cody, Surf's Up); second *thou art that*; third *he is him*. All point to the same non-duality: becoming him *is* becoming you.
6. **Cyrano/Let It Shine as cautionary tales.** Refusing to be you and hiding behind a proxy ends in death (Cyrano) or wasted time — the plot *is* the warning.
7. **Slim Shady law.** The real has to stand up; nobody wants the fake. Authenticity is not a branding choice, it's an ontological requirement.
8. **Resonant frequency.** Like buildings in earthquake tests, your amplitude is maximal only at your natural frequency. Vibing to someone else's frequency guarantees weak amplitude.

## Contrarian Take (Required by ExtractWisdom)

Miller's "fuck you, it *is* you" is bracing, but risks collapsing discernment into authenticity absolutism. Not every "that's not me" is fear in disguise — some *is* boundaries, taste, or season. The choreographer who says "that's not me" to a style that violates their body, the introvert who declines the stage that drains them, the craftsman who refuses the clout project that betrays his north star — they're not hiding an effigy, they're honoring a limit. The missing question is *which* "not me" is fear (avoiding your frequency) vs. *which* "not me" is wisdom (protecting your frequency). Without a container (community, elder, metric) to test the difference, "be you" becomes its own performance — the loudest self, not the truest.
