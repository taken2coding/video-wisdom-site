2. **Ordstirr = word-glory.** Old Norse scalds' *ordstirr* and *Havamal* ("fame of a dead man's deeds never dies") echo *kleos* — loudness + brightness as Homeric code for greatness.
3. **Envy as brake.** Fear of *phthonos*/being shamed by multitude (Agamemnon, wife's maxim, Goethe's rebuke) keeps men small and silent.
4. **Spirit built for loudness.** Man is not built for fear/cold/dark/obscurity, but for light/power/strength/vigor — scaring men is prerequisite to scaring the devil.
5. **Silencer vs. Extended Mag as life philosophy.** Two kinds of men: hide (silencer, off minimap, camping) vs. go loud (extended mag, akimbo, down range). One wastes the attachment slot.

## Contrarian Take (Required by ExtractWisdom)

Miller's "go loud, extended mag akimbo" risks glorifying loudness as *always* virtuous, but *kleos* without *arete* (skill/virtue) is just noise — Achilles' loud fame was earned by *being* the best fighter, not just shouting. Homer also praises *metis* (cunning) — Odysseus *does* negotiate with and trick his enemies, and sometimes the suppressor *is* the right attachment (ambush, patience, stealth). The missing discernment is *when* loudness is courage vs. when it's performative ruckus that just makes you an easier target for an enemy who already has wall hacks — going loud without extended mag (sustained fire, supply, training) is just camping in the open.
