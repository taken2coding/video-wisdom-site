## Summary (1 paragraph)

In 16 seconds of paradox — *Canticle of the Hollow*'s "It Only Comes to the Pure" — purity is redefined as cessation, not striving. "They say it only comes to the pure, so I stop trying to touch it, and that's when it came closer, not to be helped but to rest." The speaker stops reaching for what only comes to the pure, and in that stopping, it approaches — not to be helped (as object to fix) but to rest (as subject to shelter). The hollow's purity is not moral perfection but non-grasping rest — letting come rather than taking.

## Key Insights (Distilled)

1. **Comes to the pure, not the striving.** Trying to touch repels; stopping trying invites approach.
2. **Pure = not grasping.** Purity here is not moralism but non-appropriation — to not try to touch.
3. **Closer, not to be helped.** When it comes closer after stopping, its intent is not neediness (to be helped) but rest — it seeks shelter, not service.
4. **Hollow as resting place.** The canticle's hollow is where what comes to the pure can rest, not where it is used.

## Contrarian Take (Required by ExtractWisdom)

The canticle's "stop trying to touch it" risks quietism — sometimes what only comes to the pure *does* need to be helped, not just rested with, and stopping can become passivity that lets the pure remain untouched and alone. Not every approach should be met with rest; some should be met with work — the pure may come closer precisely to be helped, and calling that "rest" may be your own projection of hollowness.
