## Key Insights (Distilled)

1. **First hurt = first love lesson.** Who hurt you first taught you love — that timing makes the lesson feel like truth.
2. **Devotional fear, loyalty-silence, chosen-used confusions.** Three conflations born from early hurt: fear feels like devotion, silence feels like loyalty, being used feels like being chosen.
3. **Early feels sacred.** Because it came first, it feels sacred, not because it is — chronology masquerades as theology.
4. **Defend wounded, repeated.** We defend what wounded us, then repeat it — repetition is defense, not destiny.
5. **Fate vs. education.** "Then call the repetition fate. No, it was an education." — fate is just unexamined curriculum.

## Contrarian Take (Required by ExtractWisdom)

The canticle's "it was an education, not fate" is liberating, but risks dismissing that some first hurts *are* sacred — not because they were early, but because they revealed a real shape of love that, even when wounding, was truer than later, smoother loves. Not every defended repetition is mere wound-defense; some repetition is fidelity to a hard-won, early, *true* lesson that later soft educations would dilute. The missing discernment is which early lesson was wound and which was wisdom.
