## Contrarian Take (Required by ExtractWisdom)

Miller's elegant *Frogs* → Thucydides → Caesar cycle is seductive but risks historical determinism — not every Peloponnesian War leads to an Alexander, and not every American identity crisis needs a Caesar; sometimes exhaustion just stays exhaustion, or reunification comes via boring institutions, not a backwoods strongman. The *Frogs* itself is a *comedy* — Aristophanes is laughing at both Aeschylus *and* Euripides, not crowning one. Treating Aeschylus's hero worship as the corrective to Euripidean deconstruction just swaps one half-truth for the other; the Athenian lesson may be that art and war both need *both* — heroes to worship and heroes in rags — or they both die and only frogs remain.
