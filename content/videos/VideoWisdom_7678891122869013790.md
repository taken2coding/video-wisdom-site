## Summary (1 paragraph)

A 48-second canticle stages beauty as a burden to be guarded, not displayed. "Do you think I'm beautiful? I knew you'd say that. No, no, don't become jealous. Beauty is a burden." The speaker recalls a boy who said he loved her and was sent away — not from cruelty, but from a law: "If I let people love me, eventually they begin to think they know me." The interlocutor is praised for the opposite — "You never make that mistake. That is why you are my oldest friend." — because he does not mistake love for knowledge, nor beauty for availability. The final deflection — "Will you stop talking to that dirty little dog?" — reveals the true interlocutor may not be human at all, making the whole exchange a meditation on how beauty, to remain itself, must refuse to be known, and how the oldest friendship is the one that never presumes to know.

## Key Insights (Distilled)

1. **Beauty as burden, not asset.** "Beauty is a burden" — to be seen as beautiful is to carry the weight of others' projection.
2. **Love → knowing fallacy.** "If I let people love me, eventually they begin to think they know me" — love is mistaken for epistemology.
3. **Sending away as kindness.** The boy is sent away not from cruelty but to preserve the boundary between being loved and being known; cruelty would be letting him stay and believe he knows.
4. **Oldest friend = one who doesn't make that mistake.** Longevity comes from never conflating love with knowledge — the only safe intimacy.
5. **Dirty little dog as mirror.** The final line reframes the entire dialogue — the "you" may be a dog, the only being who can love without claiming to know, hence the oldest friend.
6. **Canticle of the Hollow again.** Hollowness is preserved by refusing to fill it with false knowing — beauty stays hollow so it can remain a canticle.

## Mental Models

- **Love/Know Conflation:** Love → "I know you" is the error. Keep them distinct.
- **Burden vs. Blessing:** Beauty as burden test — does being seen feel like weight or gift? If weight, you are carrying others' knowing.
- **Oldest Friend Filter:** Who has loved you longest without claiming to know you? That's your hollow-keeper.
- **Dirty Dog Lens:** The lowliest, most faithful watcher (dog) models the right distance — near, loving, not knowing.

## Actionable Steps

1. **Burden audit (today):** Where does being seen feel like a burden? Name whose "love" came with "I know you" and how you sent them away or didn't.

3. **Boundary as kindness:** Identify one person you sent away (or need to) not from cruelty but to prevent them from thinking they know you — make the boundary explicit, kindly.
4. **Dog practice:** Spend 10 minutes with an animal or child who loves without knowing — observe how that love feels different from human love that claims to know.
5. **Canticle hold:** Keep one beautiful thing private for 48 hours — don't let love turn it into "known" — let hollowness sing.

## Contrarian Take (Required by ExtractWisdom)

The canticle beautifies withholding as wisdom — "sending away" to avoid being known — but not every "I love you" that claims to know is a violation; sometimes love *does* come with knowing, and refusing it to stay hollow can become its own performance of beauty, a way to stay burden-free by never letting anyone stay long enough to know. The missing discernment is *which* love's knowing is presumptuous vs. *which* is earned through staying — the oldest friend may not be the one who never claims to know, but the one who stayed *after* claiming and was corrected.
