2. **Work changes a woman.** Reaper, fisherman — labor as identity, not just role; wide living as euphemism for many selves.
3. **Becoming anything vs. being invited.** He has become everything *except* invited — omnipotence without invitation is the hollow.
4. **Refusal to face as test.** "Would you refuse him to his face? Gladly" — courage is not abstract, it's facial, present.
5. **Surprise of self-presence.** "Here I am. What a surprise." — the shape-shifter's arrival is always surprise because he was never expected to simply *be*.
6. **Remaining as final becoming.** "I can become anything. Then try remaining yourself." — the hardest transformation is non-transformation, staying.

## Mental Models

- **Becoming vs. Remaining:** Can become anything ≠ can remain yourself. Test the second, not the first.
- **Haven't Been Invited:** What have you become that was never invited? That's the hollow to examine.
- **Work-Changes Identity:** What work made you a different woman/man? Name the reaper/fisherman in you.
- **Face Refusal:** Would you refuse to his face? If only behind back, it's not refusal.

## Contrarian Take (Required by ExtractWisdom)

The canticle frames "remaining yourself" as the ultimate becoming, but sometimes remaining is just rigidity — the reaper who refuses to become fisherman starves, and the man who can become anything *should* be celebrated for range, not tested to stay. The orchard's virtue may not be remaining, but *seasonal* becoming — reaper in harvest, fisherman at sea, orchard keeper in between — and "never invited" may be story, not fact.
